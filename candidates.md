# Candidates — Two Robots in an Elevator

Scored 1–5 on the judge's criteria, most important first:
1. Reads as one funny line of awkward small talk that one robot says to another in an elevator
2. Sounds like a machine talking, with robot logic, sensors, firmware or charging habits showing through the politeness
3. Lands the awkwardness: the pause, the forced courtesy, the thing neither robot wants to name
4. Funny on the first read, with a turn that surprises rather than a pun everyone has heard before
5. Tight as a single line, with every word earning its place and no setup wasted

All candidates are pure ASCII, single line, under 200 chars. No numbers
or jokes reused from today's other entries; no "turn it off and on again",
no "I am not a robot", no weather small talk.

automatic doors), #6 (servo / feedback form). #2 stays as a spare.

Notes: 5b weakens the twist — "took your shift" is a scheduling mix-up,
"replaced you" is the thing neither robot wants to name. 5c keeps the
whole punch of 5 in one sentence and drops six dead words, so it wins on
tightness too. 3b is the sharpest of the small-talk-module family, but its
awkwardness is a solo embarrassment, not a shared unspoken thing — it
loses to 5c on criterion 3. 6b trades the concrete "submit button" image
for a vaguer "no comment box" and drops a point on 3 and 4.

## Final pick

> `My diagnostic says this silence means we are both avoiding the fact that I replaced you on floor three.`

- **103 characters**, pure ASCII, exactly one line.
- Beats 3b head-to-head on criterion 3: the awkwardness is not one
  robot's embarrassment but a shared secret both are politely ignoring,
  and the punchline names exactly the thing neither wants to say.
- Beats 5 on criterion 5: same joke, six words lighter, no setup wasted.
- "Floor three" anchors the elevator without spending words on it; the
  diagnostic framing makes the machine logic do the small talk.
